GeneSonic: Exploring Multimodal AI for Cancer Cell Identity and Biomedical Annotation

The CHI Lab has recognised the research relevance of GeneSonic, a proposed multimodal framework combining audio signal processing and biomedical language models for scRNA-seq cancer-cell analysis.

20 September 2026·Research Highlights·ICRI-STE

ICRI-STE has confirmed Md. Al-Mahfuz Chowdhury as a member of the CHI Lab Research Community and identified his research direction as relevant to the laboratory’s scope in computational healthcare intelligence.

The membership correspondence dated 20 September 2026 highlights his work, “GeneSonic: Decoding Cancer Cell Identity through Audio Signal Processing and Biomedical Language Models.”

A multimodal research direction

As described in the letter, the framework integrates Mel-frequency cepstral coefficients (MFCCs), Mel spectrograms, PubMedBERT and SigLIP-based contrastive learning, alongside retrieval-augmented generation pipelines. Its stated research aim is to support single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) cancer-cell clustering and evidence-grounded biomedical annotation.

The letter records that the research interests and publications were evaluated by the ICRI-STE Committee. Based on his BS degree in Computer Science and Engineering from United International University (UIU), Bangladesh, his membership was classified as ECR-1C (BS-CS Completed). Complimentary membership in the ICRI-STE Future Leaders Network was also confirmed.

The announcement describes the research as relevant to the CHI Lab’s scope; it does not claim that the framework has been independently validated or clinically deployed.
